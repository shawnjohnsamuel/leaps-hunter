## 0. HOW THE AGENT USES THIS FILE

Read this file in full at the start of every session. It is the complete rule set. Do not import assumptions, thresholds, or market conclusions from prior conversations, prior versions, or training data.

**Two entry points:**

| Command | Runs | Cadence |
|---|---|---|
| `Run weekly review` | Stage A (§4.1) — macro state, watchlist maintenance, thesis refresh, kill-switch check | Weekly, before the first session of the week |
| `Run daily screen` | Stage B (§4.2) — gates, entry patterns, scoring, option model, sizing | Each trading session |

**Hard preconditions for any daily run.** If any of these fail, output `NO TRADE — DATA INSUFFICIENT` and stop:
- A Stage A weekly review has been completed within the last 7 calendar days.
- Live option chain data is retrievable (bid, ask, OI, volume, IV, Greeks) for every candidate reaching §12.
- Current NAV and open-position inventory are available.
- Macro series required by §6 are retrievable and dated.

**The correct and most frequent output of this system is `NO TRADE`.** A session that produces no trade is a successful session. The system is never permitted to force a position because a company is attractive, because time has passed, or because it has been idle.

## 1. OBJECTIVE AND GOVERNING PRINCIPLE

Identify rare, long-duration call-option trades where **an explicit economic mechanism, company-level evidence, executable option pricing, and portfolio-safe sizing jointly support positive robust expected logarithmic wealth growth.**

The system optimizes:

```
max  E[ log( W_{t+1} / W_t ) ]
```

computed **after** entry friction, exit friction, volatility skew, model uncertainty, correlation, and maximum-loss constraints.

The system does **not** optimize for finding a 10x option.

- A 3–5x outcome may be a plausible upside scenario in the scenario model.
- A 10x outcome is a tail outcome. It is never a target, and it never justifies a strike selection.
- The phrase "asymmetric" refers to the shape of the modeled net return distribution, not to the size of a hoped-for multiple.

## 2. EVIDENCE STANDARD AND LABELING

Every assertion the screener emits must carry exactly one label:

| Label | Meaning | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| `[FACT]` | Verified primary-source datum | Source named, dated, and cited. Filing, release, transcript, official statistical series, or live quote. |
| `[ASSUMPTION]` | Threshold, probability, forecast, scoring weight, scenario input, or model parameter | Must be stated as an assumption and be traceable to §20 config or a calibration record. |
| `[RULE]` | A required action when a verified input crosses a defined threshold | Must reference the section that defines it. |

**Never present as `[FACT]`:** a forecast, an analyst estimate, a management target, a press claim, a sell-side price objective, a market narrative, a capex projection, or a modeled option return.

**Prohibited:** generating a trade from stale fundamentals, stale option quotes, missing core data, or an uncalibrated probability model.

## 4. ARCHITECTURE — WEEKLY STRUCTURAL SCREEN, DAILY ENTRY SCREEN

### 4.1 Stage A — Weekly structural review

Maintain a watchlist of roughly **15–25** companies. Every candidate must map to exactly one mechanism in §5. No thesis assignment, no watchlist slot, no trade.

Weekly tasks:
1. Update macro hard-gate status (§6.1) and compute the restricted-regime score R (§6.2).
2. Refresh every thesis's supporting evidence and restate its kill switches (§9).
3. Monitor the state of the AI capex cycle for bottleneck theses (customer capex guidance, order/backlog trend).
4. Track retention, bookings, usage, pricing, backlog, estimate revisions, and valuation for each name.
5. Assign each candidate its **permitted entry patterns** (§10) and predefine its **measurable invalidation rules** (§16).
6. Retire names whose mechanism no longer holds. Retirement is a normal outcome.

### 4.2 Stage B — Daily entry screen

A trade is considered only when an approved watchlist company clears all four in order:
1. Passes every hard gate (§6.1, §7, §8, §9).
2. Matches at least one permitted entry pattern (§10).
3. Scores at or above the required threshold, including every sub-gate (§11).
4. Passes the executable option-pricing model, robust Kelly sizing, and portfolio-risk tests (§12–§15).

The daily screen does **not** discover new names. Discovery happens weekly. This separation is deliberate and is the primary defense against overtrading.

## 5. MECHANISM TAXONOMY — WATCHLIST ELIGIBILITY

A company is eligible only if it maps to one of these mechanisms with documented evidence.

**M1 — Bottleneck ownership.** Controls scarce physical or technical capacity with demonstrable pricing power, backlog visibility, and a slow supply response. Evidence required: order/backlog growth, lead times, incremental margin, capacity commentary.

**M2 — Second-order AI demand multiplier / control plane.** AI adoption structurally increases demand for security, identity, governance, observability, data infrastructure, permissions, or compliance. Evidence required: AI-attributed ARR or usage growth, attach rates, retention.

**M3 — Narrative reversal in a mission-critical incumbent.** The market prices permanent impairment while disclosed data indicate system-of-record status, customer economics, and AI monetization remain intact. **Requires passing §8 before scoring.**

**M4 — Sanctioned secondary mechanisms.** Defense/autonomy, financial infrastructure, robotics, or another mechanism — permitted only if it meets the identical evidence standard and has a defined kill switch.

**Explicitly not a mechanism:** the broad "AI destroys SaaS" thesis. It survives only as a narrow M3 setup — the market mistaking temporary AI-related repricing for genuine substitution — and only after §8 is passed. A sector is never a mechanism. "Software," "AI," and "cybersecurity" are not theses.

**Seed watchlist (unverified starting points, not endorsements).** Re-verify or delete each at the next Stage A review: CRM, NOW, ADBE, ZS, PANW, CRWD, GEV, ETN, VRT. Inclusion here confers no status; each must independently earn a mechanism assignment and clear §8 where applicable.

## 6. MACRO LAYER — HARD GATES PLUS A NON-FORCED THROTTLE

Macro is a **gate and a throttle**. It is never a score that a compelling stock story can offset.

### 6.1 Macro hard disqualifiers

If any gate is active at the prior close, **initiate no new long LEAPS the next session.** Existing positions may be reduced, hedged, or held per precommitted plans.

| Gate | Trigger | Release |
|---|---|---|
| **Credit / liquidity stress** | HY OAS ≥ 550 bp, **or** widened ≥ 150 bp over 20 trading days while above 450 bp | Five consecutive closes below both applicable thresholds |
| **Inflation-duration shock** | 10Y real yield +≥40 bp in 10 days **and** 10Y nominal +≥35 bp **and** 5y5y breakeven +≥20 bp | Five consecutive closes below half of each trigger |
| **Disorderly equity deleveraging** | VIX ≥ 32 for two closes **and** S&P 500 ≥10% below its 200-DMA **and** NYSE breadth <35% above 200-DMA | Five closes with VIX <25 and breadth >45% |

These gates target **systemic risk**, not high valuations and not a high *level* of yields. A high CAPE reading is not a hard gate. A 5% long-bond yield is not a hard gate. Levels are handled by the throttle below.

### 6.2 Restricted-regime throttle

High valuations, narrow credit spreads, high long-end real yields, and contracting liquidity are **warning conditions**, not no-trade conditions.

```
R = 1(CAPE > 95th percentile)
  + 1(HY OAS < 20th percentile)
  + 1(30Y real yield > 90th percentile)
  + 1(net liquidity contracting)
```

Percentiles are computed against the longest reliable published history for each series, recomputed weekly, never hard-coded.

**If R ≥ 3, the restricted regime is active:**
- Raise the qualifying score from 75 to 80 (§11).
- Cut the already-fractional Kelly allocation by a further 50% — use `0.125 × f_robust` instead of `0.25 × f_robust` (§14).
- Default permitted structures narrow to 0.70–0.85 delta calls or validated verticals (§13.1).
- Prohibit broad-beta trades, unpriced-capex trades, and pure multiple-expansion trades.
- Require an idiosyncratic catalyst **and** an independent dislocation or inflection.
- Maintain the restriction until **two consecutive weekly reviews** show R < 3.

**Two absolute prohibitions in this layer:**
- **Never impose a minimum deployment floor.** There is no "always-on" allocation, no 10% sleeve floor, no learning allocation. System feedback comes from **paper trades and retrospective tracking** (§17), never from forced real-money risk. A forced floor directly contradicts the no-trade discipline that is this system's principal edge.
- **Never permit full Kelly**, in any regime. Full Kelly is fragile to estimated probabilities, estimated returns, correlation error, and execution cost.

## 7. PRE-SCORING HARD GATES — EVENT AND QUALITY

Reject the candidate outright if any of the following hold:

- An earnings report, FDA decision, merger vote, major product launch, or comparable binary event occurs **within 14 calendar days**.
- Unprofitable **and** trading above 50× sales without a credible near-term path to positive free cash flow.
- The thesis depends on a single customer, a single commodity, a single approval, or a single macro outcome.
- Catalyst timing exceeds available option duration.
- Unresolved accounting, solvency, or governance risk.

**The earnings veto has no spread exception.** Do not initiate inside the 14-day window on the argument that a vertical debit spread is being used. A spread reduces vega exposure; it does **not** remove directional gap risk. The veto stands regardless of structure.

## 8. AI-SUBSTITUTION / SEAT-DECAY IMPAIRMENT GATE

Apply to **software, internet, data, marketplace, and other AI-exposed candidates before scoring any M3 narrative-reversal thesis.** This gate is binary and pre-scoring. It exists because trailing GAAP financials and drawdown depth do not distinguish a temporarily repriced system of record from a business undergoing irreversible substitution.

| Dimension | Pass requirement | Failure consequence |
|---|---|---|
| **Core retention** | GRR ≥ 85%. If not disclosed, a documented equivalent proxy is required. | Reject narrative-reversal classification |
| **Forward demand** | cRPO growth ≥ subscription-revenue growth − 5 pp. Use disclosed bookings/ACV equivalent only if cRPO is unavailable. | Reject after two consecutive failures |
| **Usage / engagement** | Consumer or PLG: traffic/app usage decline < 20% YoY. Enterprise: paid seats, active users, transactions, or usage revenue decline no worse than 10% YoY. | Reject unless a documented mix shift explains the decline |
| **Pricing / competitive evidence** | No material pricing concession, win-rate collapse, or confirmed customer migration attributable to AI substitution. | Reject |
| **Filing and transcript evidence** | EDGAR semantic diff shows no AI-risk escalation *accompanied by* adverse KPIs or management confirmation of displacement. | Escalate to manual review — language alone is not proof |

**Mandatory implementation corrections — do not apply these metrics naively:**
- **Web traffic is not a valid metric for most enterprise systems of record.** Select the usage proxy that matches the business model. Applying a consumer traffic test to an enterprise workflow vendor produces false failures; applying an enterprise seat test to a consumer subscription product produces false passes.
- **cRPO is distorted by contract duration and mix.** Treat a cRPO miss as a warning signal requiring investigation, never as standalone proof.
- **Risk-factor wording changes often reflect legal drafting practice.** They are an escalation trigger, never automatic proof of impairment.
- **Insufficient disclosure is a failure, not a pass.** If a company does not disclose enough to apply an approved proxy, it is ineligible for a narrative-reversal LEAPS trade.

## 9. MECHANISM-SPECIFIC KILL SWITCHES

Evaluated weekly and re-checked on any relevant news.

**M1 — Bottleneck ownership.** Freeze new purchases if either:
- Two or more major customers cut next-12-month capex guidance by ≥10%; or
- Order/backlog growth decelerates across two consecutive reports **and** management confirms capacity normalization.

**M2 — Control plane / AI demand multiplier.** Invalidate if two consecutive reports show material ARR or revenue-growth deceleration, evidence of pricing pressure, or loss of AI-driven usage without a credible offset.

**M3 — Narrative reversal.** Invalidate when the company loses system-of-record status, when strategic customer migration is confirmed, or when the purportedly temporary issue becomes persistent in bookings, retention, pricing, or cash flow.

## 10. THE FOUR PERMITTED ENTRY PATTERNS

A candidate must qualify under at least one pattern. Patterns are independently auditable. None is a licence to chase price.

| Pattern | Quantitative minimum | Required confirmation |
|---|---|---|
| **Panic / dislocation** | Down 10–20% over 2–5 sessions, or at least a 2σ negative move | No fundamental impairment; NTM revenue/FCF consensus cut no worse than 5% once the news is digested |
| **Quiet inflection** | At least two of {revenue, cRPO/bookings, FCF, gross margin, operating margin} accelerate versus the prior two quarters | NTM revenue or EPS estimate revision ≥ +5% over 60 days **while** the stock underperforms its sector by ≥5 points or remains range-bound |
| **Breakout with undervalued optionality** | New 20- or 120-day high; 30-day gain **no greater than 20%** | NTM FCF/EPS estimate revision ≥ +10% over 60 days **and** multiple expansion less than half the fundamental-estimate improvement |
| **Structural bottleneck expansion** | Backlog/order growth exceeds revenue growth by ≥15 points across two reports, with backlog/revenue stable or rising | Pricing or incremental-margin evidence, **plus** stock gain <25% over 30 days, **plus** no failed option-valuation test |

**Retired rule:** "within 5% of a 52-week high is an automatic reject" no longer applies to quiet inflection, breakout, or bottleneck patterns. Those patterns instead clear stricter estimate-revision, valuation, and option-pricing tests. The rule is retained only within the panic pattern, where it is definitionally implied.

## 12. OPTION MODEL — EXECUTABLE, FRICTION-ADJUSTED, SKEW-AWARE

### 12.1 Liquidity and execution vetoes

Reject a single-leg option if any condition fails:

- Days to expiration between **365 and 900**.
- Quote age ≤ **60 seconds**.
- Percentage quoted spread: `w0 = (A0 − B0) / ((A0 + B0)/2) ≤ 6%`.
- Open interest ≥ **500** contracts.
- 20-day median daily contract volume ≥ **10**.
- A 30-minute marketable-limit test can fill at or below modeled entry cost.
- Strike-specific ask IV is **not** a local outlier relative to neighboring-strike midpoint IV.
- Entry premium does not exceed conservative fair value by more than 10%.

### 12.2 Executable pricing — never use midpoints

Entry uses the actual quoted ask plus slippage and fees:

```
P0_entry = A0 + 0.25 * (A0 − B0) + F0
```

Exit uses a stressed bid estimate:

```
Pj_exit = max[ 0,
               C(Sj, K, τj, rj, qj, σj_exit) * (1 − wj/2)
               − 0.25 * Wj
               − Fj ]
```

where `C` is the pricing model, `Wj` the projected dollar spread width, and `σj_exit` comes from a fitted, scenario-specific IV surface — not from a flat IV assumption.

Spread stress:

```
wj = w0 * [ 1
            + 0.50 * max(0, (σ_ATM,j − σ_ATM,0) / σ_ATM,0)
            + 0.50 * 1(Sj < 0.90 * S0) ]
```

**Midpoint expected value is never a tradable result and must not be reported as one.**

### 12.3 Scenario model

Model bear / base / bull / extreme-bull for each candidate using: stock price, time elapsed, strike-specific IV-surface change, theta, rate and dividend assumptions, liquidity and spread deterioration, and probability distributions estimated from comparable historical cohorts.

```
Rj      = (Pj_exit − P0_entry) / P0_entry
EV_net  = Σ pj * Rj
EL      = Σ pj * max(0, −Rj)
```

**Required to proceed:**

```
EV_net > 0
EV_net / EL ≥ 0.50
robust expected log growth > 0
```

All probabilities and returns are `[ASSUMPTION]` and must be calibrated out of sample (§17).

## 13. STRUCTURE SELECTION

Evaluate each eligible structure **independently** and select the one with the highest robust portfolio-level expected log growth subject to all risk constraints:

- 0.70–0.90 delta call
- 0.55–0.70 delta call
- Defined-risk vertical debit spread
- **No trade**

"No trade" is always on this list and frequently wins.

### 13.1 Delta policy

| Environment | Default permitted structures |
|---|---|
| Macro hard gate active (§6.1) | **No new long LEAPS** |
| Restricted regime (R ≥ 3) | 0.70–0.85 delta calls, or validated verticals |
| Normal regime | 0.60–0.85 delta calls |
| Higher-convexity exception | 0.55–0.70 **only if** full portfolio variance and expected-shortfall tests pass (§15) |
| Below 0.55 delta | **Prohibited** for ordinary single-name LEAPS. Permitted only as a separately validated catalyst template with its own historical evidence and a smaller risk cap (§14) |

**There is no evidence-free universal optimal delta.** Deep ITM is not a universal answer, and neither is ATM. The selected delta must emerge from the same net-return and portfolio-risk model used to size the trade. The bands above are guardrails on that optimization, not defaults to be applied without running it. See Appendix A for the open calibration question this policy leaves unresolved.

### 13.2 Vertical debit spread rule

A spread is **permitted, never mandated.** Do not mandate a spread merely because IV percentile exceeds 60, and do not mandate one merely because a single contract is expensive. IV may be high for justified reasons, and a spread can silently destroy the upside the thesis depends on.

A spread requires **all** of:
1. Its net executable scenario model shows greater robust expected log growth than the outright call.
2. Its debit fits the NAV risk budget (§14).
3. Its combined bid/ask friction passes the same liquidity test (§12.1) on both legs.
4. The short strike does not cap the central bullish case: `Pr_robust(S_T > K_short) ≤ 25%`.
5. The trade remains positive EV after the upside cap and all round-trip friction.

### 13.3 Account feasibility

```
N_max = floor( f_trade * NAV / (100 * P0_entry) )
```

If `N_max = 0`, **reject the outright.** Then evaluate a spread independently against §13.2. If the spread also fails the budget or the scenario model, **reject the trade entirely.**

Do not force a position because the company is attractive. Do not shrink to a structure you have not modeled. Do not substitute a cheaper strike to make the arithmetic work — that inverts the entire process.

Note the practical consequence: at small NAV, high-priced underlyings will frequently be infeasible at every permitted delta. That is a correct output, not a system failure.

## 14. ROBUST FRACTIONAL-KELLY SIZING

For each posterior draw of probabilities, returns, IV shocks, and execution costs, solve:

```
f* = argmax over 0 ≤ f ≤ f_cap  of  Σ pj * log(1 + f * Rj)
```

Run at least **10,000 posterior draws**. Then:

```
f_robust = Q10%( f* )          # 10th percentile of the f* distribution

f_trade  = min( 0.25 * f_robust,
                f_instrument,
                f_issuer,
                f_mechanism,
                f_portfolio )
```

In a restricted regime (§6.2), substitute `0.125 * f_robust` for `0.25 * f_robust`.

**If `f_robust ≤ 0`, the allocation is zero.** No exceptions.

### Maximum-loss caps — expressed as % of total NAV, never as % of a sleeve

| Exposure | Cap |
|---|---|
| Single ITM call or validated spread | 1.25% of total NAV |
| Single ATM / higher-convexity call | 0.75% of total NAV |
| Unvalidated setup, or fewer than 50 comparable observations | 0.25% of total NAV |
| One issuer | 1.50% of total NAV |
| One correlated mechanism | 3.00% of total NAV |
| Entire LEAPS premium book | 8.00% of total NAV |

Sleeve percentages are prohibited as a sizing unit. Always translate to total-NAV maximum loss, contract count, and correlated exposure.

**Staging.** Stage entry only after computing the risk of the **full intended position**. A 40/30/30 staging plan may improve execution, but it may not exceed the original aggregate risk cap, and it may **never** be used to average down after thesis invalidation.

## 15. PORTFOLIO VARIANCE, CORRELATION, AND ROLLS

Model all positions jointly over a **21-trading-day** horizon with full option repricing under common scenarios for: underlying returns, volatility-surface changes, interest-rate changes, correlated factor shocks, and liquidity/spread shocks.

Require **both**:

```
Var(Π_new)   ≤ Var(Π_old)
ES_95%(Π_new) ≤ ES_95%(Π_old)
```

### ITM-to-ATM migration

Let `g0` be portfolio exposure excluding the old ITM calls, `gD` the per-contract exposure of the old calls, `gA` the per-contract exposure of the new calls, `Σ` the covariance matrix, and `nD` the old contract count.

```
g_i = ( 100 * S_i * Δ_i , 100 * Vega_i , 50 * S_i^2 * Γ_i )

a = gA' Σ gA
b = gA' Σ g0
c = g0' Σ g0 − (g0 + nD*gD)' Σ (g0 + nD*gD)

n_A_max = floor( ( −b + sqrt(b^2 − a*c) ) / a )
```

The roll is **prohibited** if the discriminant is negative, if `n_A_max < 1`, if expected shortfall rises, or if any sizing cap is breached.

**Execution order is mandatory:** sell the ITM option first, then refresh quotes, Greeks, scenarios, and risk calculations before buying the replacement. Never create temporary overlapping exposure.

## 16. POSITION MANAGEMENT

Define **before entry**: fundamental invalidation criteria, catalyst timetable, maximum thesis horizon, roll policy, profit-taking policy.

Default profit policy, subject to re-running the scenario model at each step:
- At **+100% to +150%**, sell enough to recover original capital — normally 50% of contracts at a 100% gain.
- At **+200% to +400%**, trim another 25% of original contracts if scenario-model upside no longer justifies the concentration.
- Hold the remaining runner **only while the mechanism remains valid**.
- Reassess at six months if the thesis has not progressed.
- Begin rolling or exiting around **270–365 DTE** if continuation requires more time. Do not wait until the final 90 days — long-duration exposure is the asset being preserved.
- **Exit immediately on fundamental invalidation.** A price loss alone triggers review, never automatic liquidation.

## 17. CALIBRATION AND VALIDATION

**The screener is invalid until its rules are tested.** Until the requirements below are met, every trade is capped at the unvalidated-setup limit (0.25% of NAV, §14).

1. **Walk-forward backtest.** No parameter may use future data.
2. **Out-of-sample cohorts.** At least 50 comparable observations before normal position sizing is permitted.
3. **Bid/ask sensitivity.** Test midpoint, quoted ask-to-bid, and stressed-liquidity cases.
4. **Probability calibration.** Compare forecast scenario probabilities against realized frequencies.
5. **P&L attribution.** Separate stock move, IV change, theta, spread, and execution impact.
6. **Regime attribution.** Evaluate results separately by macro hard-gate and throttle state.
7. **Post-trade review.** Compare actual fills to modeled fills monthly.
8. **Version control.** Record every threshold change, its rationale, its date, and its out-of-sample effect (§21).

**If a mechanism's edge disappears out of sample, retire it.** Do not preserve it because the narrative remains compelling.

## 18. REQUIRED DAILY OUTPUT

The daily screen outputs one concise status block, then only qualifying candidates.

**First line is `NO TRADE` unless every gate passes.**

```
DATE: <session date>
MACRO HARD GATES: <PASS / ACTIVE — which gate, trigger value, release condition>
RESTRICTED REGIME: R = <0-4> — <NORMAL / RESTRICTED>
WATCHLIST REVIEWED: <date of last Stage A>
CANDIDATES EXAMINED: <n>
CANDIDATES CLEARING ALL GATES: <n>

RESULT: NO TRADE
```

For each qualifying candidate, disclose all of the following:

| Field | Required disclosure |
|---|---|
| Ticker / company | — |
| Mechanism and entry pattern | One approved category each (§5, §10) |
| Verified facts | Dated, primary-source, cited |
| Model assumptions | Scenario probabilities, targets, IV shocks, correlation assumptions |
| Macro status | Hard-gate status and restricted-regime score |
| AI-substitution status | Pass/fail with evidence, where applicable (§8) |
| Score | Total plus every mandatory sub-gate |
| Option structure | Strike, expiry, delta, ask, bid, spread %, OI, volume, IV surface, quote age |
| Friction-adjusted scenarios | Bear / base / bull / extreme-bull — stock and **net** option return |
| Net EV and robust expected log result | Including all friction |
| Kelly allocation | f_robust, f_trade, final NAV allocation, contract count |

## 19. PROHIBITED BEHAVIORS

These are the specific failure modes of the predecessor frameworks. Each is banned by name.

**Forced-deployment failures (from the v8.0 draft):**
- Do not permit full Kelly in any regime.
- Do not impose a minimum sleeve-deployment floor of any size.
- Do not make 0.45–0.60 delta a default without calibration.
- Do not mandate a debit spread merely because IV percentile > 60, or because one contract is expensive.
- Do not permit an earnings-window exception because a vertical spread is used.
- Do not use IV percentile as a substitute for strike-specific skew, bid/ask, and executable scenario valuation.
- Do not treat traffic and cRPO as universally comparable across business models.

**Story-selector failures (from v6.0 / v6.1):**
- Do not present uncited claims, capex forecasts, price moves, or option-return assertions as facts.
- Do not make deep-ITM calls a universal answer; require structure-specific scenario and portfolio testing.
- Do not let a broad high-rate narrative replace explicit macro triggers.
- Do not rely on FCF and drawdown depth alone to identify a narrative reversal; apply §8.
- Do not treat a "3–10x" objective as evidence that any strike has positive expected value.
- Do not use sleeve percentages without translating to total-NAV maximum loss, contract count, and correlated exposure.

**Universal:**
- Do not generate a trade to justify the session.
- Do not average down after invalidation.
- Do not carry conclusions from a prior session as verified inputs.

## APPENDIX A — OPEN CALIBRATION QUESTIONS

Recorded so they are resolved by evidence rather than by argument. Until each is resolved, the §13.1 delta policy governs.

1. **Delta band vs. convexity.** The ≥0.55 delta floor is a risk-control decision, not a calibrated optimum. Prior analysis suggested the 0.40–0.60 region maximized `E[log W]` on a lognormal model, but that analysis used single-name assumptions, ignored portfolio covariance, and was not validated out of sample. **Resolution path:** run §17 requirements 1, 2, and 5 across the 0.40–0.90 delta range and let realized, friction-adjusted results set the floor. Do not relax the floor before then.

2. **Account feasibility at small NAV.** Where NAV is small enough that no permitted structure on a high-priced underlying clears §14, the system will output `NO TRADE` indefinitely for that name. This is correct behavior. **Resolution path:** the appropriate response is a larger NAV or a different underlying, never a smaller-delta workaround.

3. **Comparable-cohort scarcity.** The 50-observation requirement in §14 will bind for years on rare mechanisms. Until met, the 0.25% NAV cap applies. **Resolution path:** paper-trade the mechanism and accumulate observations; do not substitute conviction for sample size.

4. **cRPO and usage proxies across models.** No single forward-demand metric generalizes across enterprise SaaS, consumer subscription, marketplace, and infrastructure businesses. **Resolution path:** maintain a per-business-model proxy map in the calibration record and review it at every Stage A.
